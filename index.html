The Case Study

Behind the Bellabeat Dataset

Bellabeat designs wellness technology for women — trackers, jewelry, and an app that turns daily biometrics into guidance a person can act on. This study works from an anonymized export of thirty-five wearable users, with per-member activity totals joined against sleep logs on a shared member ID.

Every figure on this page is pulled directly from that join: daily step and distance averages, active-minute breakdowns, calories, and — for the members who logged it — minutes asleep versus minutes simply lying in bed. My job was to clean it, question it, and ask what it does and doesn't tell Bellabeat's product team about the women using their devices.

Three Key Insights

What the Numbers Say

Three findings, drawn straight from the combined activity-and-sleep table, that Bellabeat's product and marketing teams could act on this quarter.

I.

of tracked minutes, sedentary

Movement is the exception, not the norm

Sedentary minutes average 989 a day against just 32 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity. Average daily steps sit at 6,538 — and only 17% of members clear the 10,000-step mark even once as an average.

II.

of members logged any sleep

Sleep is good when it's captured — the gap is capture itself

Among the 24 members who logged sleep, efficiency averages 91.2% — only 42 minutes a night spent awake in bed. The story isn't poor sleep. It's that a third of members never logged a night at all.

III.

of members had a zero-step day

Engagement dips are common — and activity still pays off

Steps and calories burned move together with a moderate positive correlation (r ≈ 0.48), so consistent movement measurably shows up in energy spend. But 14 of 35 members had at least one fully untracked, zero-step day — 61 such days in total.

Where the Data Falls Short

Four Areas That Need More Data

A confident analysis is honest about its blind spots. Here's what I'd ask Bellabeat to collect next, in order of how much it would change the story.

Incomplete sleep logging

11 of 35 members — 31% — never logged a single night of sleep. Any sleep-related conclusion is built on two-thirds of the population; the rest is a silent gap, not a "zero."

No demographic context

Age, height, weight, and menstrual-cycle phase aren't in this export. Without them, every insight is an average across a population Bellabeat can't yet segment or personalize for.

A short observation window

Members were tracked for 8 to 32 days, averaging just 13. That's enough to see a snapshot, not enough to separate a habit from a busy week or a holiday.

No heart rate or stress markers

Resting heart rate, HRV, and stress scores — signals many wearables capture — are absent here, so recovery and strain can only be inferred from steps and sleep, never confirmed.

Looking Ahead

Three Predictions for Bellabeat

Reasonable, data-grounded forecasts — not guarantees — for what happens if these patterns continue or are acted on.

Activity Nudges

Small step gains should show up in calorie burn

Given the steps-to-calories relationship already in the data, a nudge that lifts the average member from ~6,500 to ~8,000 steps a day is likely to register as a measurable, reportable rise in energy expenditure — a believable in-app milestone, not a moonshot.

Retention Signal

Logging gaps will keep tracking engagement, not just sleep

The members who skip sleep logging are, almost by definition, the lighter app users. Expect sleep-logging rate to keep functioning as an early warning sign for churn — worth watching in its own right, separate from actual sleep quality.

Personalization

Filling the demographic gap will unlock real segmentation

Once age, cycle phase, and longer history are collected, I'd expect today's single "average member" story to split into distinct groups — likely with different step, sleep, and recovery norms — each deserving its own guidance rather than one blended target.
